# CP00 / COPAUA0C messaging audit

- This is the derive-lane (#2) audit of COPAUA0C, the program behind the CP00 card-authorization request over MQ. It records how a request is consumed and parsed, the lookups and the summary lock, the decision with the i10 guard, the reply body byte by byte and its properties, the ordering of reply and writes, the database effects and the error-log rows.
- It comes before `modern/crates/domain/src/messaging/copaua0c.rs`, `modern/crates/messaging/src/workers/cp00.rs`, `persistence::repos::pending_auth_summaries::lock_or_create` and the tests that prove them. Code that consumes it is accepted only once the reviewer marks this file `Accepted` in the status table of `modern/audit/README.md` (a8, a9; AAP 0.8.6).
- Every source was read in place at commit `59cc6c2` and none was changed (R16).

## Ordering decision and its exposure (E29)

- COPAUA0C puts the reply with `MQPUT1` and `MQPMO-NO-SYNCPOINT`, outside the unit of work (`COPAUA0C.cbl:753-758`), before `8000-WRITE-AUTH-TO-DB` (`COPAUA0C.cbl:459-465`); its IMS writes commit only at the `SYNCPOINT` after each message (`COPAUA0C.cbl:334-336`). The replacement keeps that order by decision (AAP 0.3.4.2): rows COPAUA0C-080 to COPAUA0C-082 and COPAUA0C-117 to COPAUA0C-121.
- The exposure that order carries, in the terms of AAP 0.3.4.2 and 0.8.6:
  - a delivered decision can go unrecorded: after an `I003` summary-write failure, after a commit failure, or after a process or connection loss that follows the acknowledgement;
  - an `I004` detail-insert failure commits the summary without its detail;
  - i10 counts committed approvals only, so the account's real exposure can exceed the committed figure by the approvals replied but never recorded.
- E29 stays `AWAITING SIGNATURE` until the `E29 — CP00 reply before write` row of `../README.md` carries an `Accepted` decision with the reviewer's name and date. This file states the decision and does not accept it.

## Not carried over

- These COBOL paths have no rule row, so a8 demands no test for them; each has the reason given.
- `EXEC CICS RETRIEVE` of the trigger message (`COPAUA0C.cbl:233-240`). The worker is a long-running AMQP consumer started by the operator, not a triggered CICS task, and its request queue comes from configuration.

- The `M003` record `FAILED TO READ REQUEST MQ` (`COPAUA0C.cbl:415-430`). It is replaced by the consumer-loss outcome of AAP 0.3.4.2 (COPAUA0C-118), with no `M003` row. Its one cause an AMQP request could still present, a body over 500 bytes, is COPAUA0C-010 and X-COPAUA0C-5.
